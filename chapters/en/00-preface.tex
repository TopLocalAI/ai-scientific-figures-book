\chapter*{Preface: Putting Attention Back on Scientific Communication}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Preface}
\markboth{Preface}{Preface}

Many researchers only confront their paper figures at the most frantic moment of a project. Laboratory measurements, field campaigns, or numerical simulations have just wrapped up; the manuscript text is on its third draft; co-authors are pressing to finalize the file; a draft submission is already sitting open in the journal portal; or a conference abstract deadline is only days away. At that point, folders full of disparate files get opened at once. A graduate student in environmental science stares at a full year of river-monitoring spreadsheets, trying to pack discharge pulses and nitrate concentration responses into a single figure that is neither cramped nor misleading. A student in materials and energy finishes assembling a four-panel manuscript composite and pastes the exact same image onto a group-meeting slide and a conference poster, only to realize that colleagues in the back row cannot even read the axis units. Meanwhile, a doctoral candidate in biomedicine or engineering looks at the seventh revision of a mechanism diagram and receives a margin note from an advisor that offers no actionable starting point: ``Still not clear enough---make it look more professional.''

Why does figure preparation so often turn into a bottleneck at the finish line? Because making a scientific figure is never merely an exercise in software mechanics or graphic polish; it is an unavoidable sequence of decisions about research communication. The moment you commit a study to a bounded page, you have to answer four questions on the spot: What matters most in this work? Which elements are measured observations or experimental evidence, and which are mechanistic explanations, working hypotheses, or unverified inferences? Are the relationships among objects causal, correlational, sequential in time, or hierarchical in space? And in what order should a reader scan the page to grasp the central contribution without misreading the claim? Ambiguities that can be smoothed over with a hedged sentence in the main text are exposed immediately once they have to be drawn as axes, arrows, colour roles, and panel labels. Many difficulties that look like ``not knowing how to use illustration software'' trace back to a more fundamental issue: the research argument itself has not yet been decomposed clearly in the author's mind (Rougier et al., 2014; Weissgerber et al., 2015).

Generative artificial intelligence and AI-assisted drawing tools have genuinely changed where scientific figure making begins. Tasks that once required hours or days of repetitive manual labour---drafting a mechanism schematic from scratch, comparing three multi-panel layouts, testing alternative chart forms for the same dataset, or outlining a complete multi-panel legend---can now start from a short written description, a rough hand-drawn sketch, a reference image, or a cleaned data table. Turning an idea into a visual draft for discussion takes minutes rather than days, lowering the cost of exploration and comparison. At the same time, AI has not changed the foundational baseline of scientific illustration: full scientific responsibility for every figure rests with the authors. A model cannot answer for your experimental design, verify whether an arrow in the literature represents direct catalytic activation or an indirect association, or absorb the academic integrity risk of upscaling raw microscopy, inventing sample sizes, or smoothing away outliers. On the contrary, because generative models routinely produce harmonious palettes and smooth contours, scientific errors hidden beneath a polished surface---data points redrawn at altered coordinates, transport arrows pointing in the wrong direction, scale bars lacking calibration metadata, or qualifying conditions quietly cropped out---are often much harder to catch at a glance than flaws in a rough hand sketch.

For that reason, this book is not a catalogue of ``magic prompts,'' nor does it treat AI as a black box that turns a one-sentence request into a submission-ready figure. A single working method runs through every chapter: \textbf{write a structured figure brief first, choose the right workflow and tools second, perform verifiable checks at every step, and deliver a traceable archive at the end.} In each chapter, we begin by translating a concrete research question into a structured figure brief, separating tasks that benefit from AI ideation or drafting from tasks that require deterministic plotting scripts, vector typesetting layers, or read-only raw-image workflows. Every chapter explains explicitly what AI can do at that stage, what it cannot do, and what the author must verify against the research record, closing with a side-by-side ``What AI Can Do / Cannot Do / Author Checks'' reference table and an actionable verification checklist.

So that you can carry the same research cases all the way through ``concept---sketch---finished figure---multi-panel assembly---talk and poster adaptation---technical check---handoff archive'' without jumping across a dozen disconnected programs, the book uses \scidraw{} as a unified practical demonstration platform. Every workflow covered in the text---the figure workflow guide, prompt drawing, sketch mode, reference-image editing, mechanism figure generation, workflow diagram generation, graphical abstract creation, conversational scientific data visualization, multi-panel figure assembly, figure legend assistance, and automated technical figure checks---is based on hands-on testing of features live during writing (through October 29, 2026). We describe tools candidly: where a module completes a step directly, we provide reproducible inputs and settings; where a workflow requires downloading local backups from the browser, separating a raster base illustration from a vector label layer, switching to a deterministic plotting script to obtain true vector line art, or manually supplying experimental conditions and statistical test results, we state those boundaries and fallback procedures explicitly. Interface screenshots reflect the dated test build, and subsequent product updates are documented in the companion repository. More importantly, the core discipline taught here---decomposing research into a figure brief, establishing a consistent visual language, grading evidence status, and auditing deliverables---is not tied to any single software package and transfers directly to other graphics editors or code-based workflows.

Part I, ``Clarify the Research Argument First'' (Chapters 1--4), covers the preparation before drawing begins: Chapter 1 establishes the six-step workflow and delivers a one-sentence goal, a data dictionary, and a compact project archive; Chapter 2 decomposes research content into a ten-field figure brief, a structured relationship table, and a reverse-reading check; Chapter 3 works through composition and rough sketches, delivering three spatial skeletons, a layout comparison matrix, and a single-target revision log; and Chapter 4 builds a paper-wide visual language, delivering semantic colour roles, a typographic hierarchy, a line-style vocabulary, and a one-page visual specification sheet. Part II, ``Build Specific Types of Scientific Figures'' (Chapters 5--10), addresses the major figure genres: Chapter 5 covers data visualization, delivering distribution-comparison plots with explicit error-bar and sample-size definitions alongside a numerical verification log; Chapter 6 covers methods and workflow diagrams, delivering a parameterized step table and a two-lane workflow diagram with explicit quality-control exits; Chapter 7 covers mechanism and structure schematics, delivering pathway and cross-sectional figures built from text-free base illustrations paired with vector labels; Chapter 8 covers experimental image presentation, delivering raw-image calibration records, colour-vision-accessible pseudocolour composites, and verified scale bars; Chapter 9 organizes multi-panel paper figures, delivering a panel-role allocation table, a publication layout grid, and an author-verified figure legend; and Chapter 10 focuses on graphical abstracts, delivering a four-line contribution card and a table-of-contents thumbnail legibility test. Part III, ``Revise, Adapt, and Deliver'' (Chapters 11--13), handles iteration, reuse, and final handoff: Chapter 11 turns vague critique into executable revisions prioritized across scientific errors, reading barriers, and visual consistency, delivering pixel-difference audit views and a version tree; Chapter 12 adapts manuscript composite figures into widescreen talk slides and an A0 conference poster, delivering a viewing-angle font-size table and a three-version consistency check; and Chapter 13 completes pre-submission technical verification and archiving, delivering effective-resolution calculations, vector-container inspections, colour-vision deficiency simulations, and a self-contained handoff package.

To give readers from diverse fields familiar scientific ground, the book draws on cross-disciplinary case studies: Earth and environmental sciences feature public US Geological Survey (USGS) daily river nitrate and discharge monitoring records from an agricultural watershed (Chapter 1) alongside global ocean heat content and atmospheric carbon dioxide observational records (Chapters 4 and 5); ecology and biomedicine span Palmer Archipelago penguin morphometrics (Chapter 5), a murine lung droplet single-cell RNA-sequencing workflow (Chapter 6), CRISPR--Cas9 genome-editing repair pathways (Chapter 3), insulin-stimulated GLUT4 vesicle translocation signalling (Chapters 7 and 11), and public fluorescence microscopy and leaf-epidermis scanning electron micrographs (Chapter 8); materials, energy, and engineering cover lithium-ion battery capacity-fade mechanisms and pouch-cell cross-sections (Chapters 2 and 7), an end-to-end thread on planar perovskite solar cell interfacial passivation running from a manuscript composite figure through talk slides, an A0 poster, and final delivery checks (Chapters 9, 12, and 13), a satellite remote-sensing burned-area segmentation graphical abstract (Chapter 10), and a cable-stayed bridge structural health monitoring network schematic (Chapter 11), complemented by an eight-subject paired-measurement simulated dataset and the abstract R/K/P/I regulatory network model for foundational exercises. The rationale for crossing disciplines is straightforward: although the physical form of evidence varies from field to field, the underlying logic of scientific communication---separating observation from interpretation, controlling information density, and locking immutable constraints---is shared across all of them.

When reading and reusing materials from this book, please keep four academic integrity commitments in mind. First, every simulated dataset used for teaching demonstrations is explicitly labelled ``Simulated data'' both inside the figure and in the figure legend, and must never be presented as empirical laboratory or field measurements. Second, every structural motif or morphological placeholder generated by an image model is explicitly marked ``Model-generated illustration, not a micrograph'' or ``Schematic, not to scale,'' without fabricated scale bars or instrument data strips. Third, for real experimental images, we maintain a strict boundary between full-frame linear intensity adjustments and pixel-altering generative edits, never applying generative beautification, inpainting, or AI super-resolution upscaling to primary evidence images. Fourth, this book never claims that any AI-generated figure is ``ready for direct submission''; because journals, conferences, and institutions update their policies on AI-assisted illustration and disclosure frequently, you must always consult the official author guidelines of your target venue and institution before submission.

You can read the book sequentially from Chapter 1 to Chapter 13, or jump directly along one of the three task-based paths shown in Figure~\ref{fig:fm-01}: if you are assembling a manuscript composite figure and its legend under a deadline, start with Chapters 2, 5, 9, and 13; if you are preparing a group-meeting presentation or a conference poster, read Chapters 2, 4, 12, and 13; if you are designing a mechanism schematic or an experimental workflow diagram, follow Chapters 2, 3, 6, 7, 11, and 13. Framed \texttt{Prompt} boxes throughout the text hold reusable figure briefs, prompts, and revision instructions that you can copy and adapt to your own parameters; indented \texttt{BookNote} callouts highlight operational boundaries and pitfalls; and evidence-status labels inside figures and legends are integral scientific qualifiers that must be retained whenever a figure is reused across formats. Seven appendices at the back of the book compile figure-brief templates, a vague-feedback rewrite table, a twelve-point figure-legend checklist, a pre-submission delivery checklist, a glossary of core terms, a master table of case and asset sources, and a quick-reference guide to dimensions and font sizes. Before turning to Chapter 1, pick one figure on your desk that has been giving you trouble, gather its underlying data table or rough sketch, and set aside a few quiet minutes to decompose the problem. From the next page onward, shift your focus temporarily away from ``generating a polished image'' toward a single, concrete question: What should this figure help the reader understand?
